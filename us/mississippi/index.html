Mississippi sports betting at a glance

Mississippi was one of the first US states to legalize sports betting after PASPA fell in May 2018, launching retail sportsbooks at its riverboat and coastal casinos in August 2018. Mobile has been debated ever since — every legislative session between 2019 and 2025 saw a mobile bill filed. HB 774 finally passed in 2025 with mobile authorization, and the Mississippi Gaming Commission is targeting fall 2026 for launch.

Population: 2.9 million. Legal mobile sports betting: no. Legal retail sports betting: yes. Regulator: Mississippi Gaming Commission (MGC).. Tax rate on gross gaming revenue: 12% on adjusted gross gaming revenue (mobile authorized at same rate)..

Market-size projections matter more than headline numbers because they explain the political economy of legalization. States with large projected markets attract more operator lobbying, more legislative attention, and — often — faster passage once the political conditions align. States with smaller projected markets can pass legislation with less controversy but generate less state revenue post-launch.

Line shopping and hold rates in Mississippi

With multiple operators licensed, Mississippi residents can and should line-shop every wager. The price difference between operators on a given line can be 1-5 cents on sides and 5-15 cents on props, which compounds meaningfully across a season of betting.

What is the sports-betting tax rate in Mississippi?

12% on adjusted gross gaming revenue (mobile authorized at same rate).. This is a tax on the operator's gross gaming revenue, not on individual player winnings.

Do I have to pay taxes on my Mississippi sports-betting winnings?

Yes. Winnings are taxable income at the federal level (reportable on IRS Form W-2G for wins above operator-reporting thresholds) and typically at the state level in the state where the wager was placed. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.
