How casino welcome bonuses actually work

Casino welcome bonuses run on dramatically different economics from sportsbook bonuses. Three structural differences are essential to understand before you claim anything.

First, the math of rollover. A 15x rollover on a $1,000 bonus means you must wager $15,000 in qualifying play before any portion of that bonus or its winnings becomes withdrawable cash. Different game types contribute different percentages — slots typically count 100% (so $15,000 of slot play satisfies the requirement), table games count 10-20% (so $75,000-$150,000 of table-game play would be required for the same clearance), and live dealer games typically count somewhere in between. The realized expected value of a casino bonus depends almost entirely on how efficiently you can clear that rollover with games that have favorable RTP and contribute fully to the requirement.

Second, the bonus-only-funds vs cashable-bonus distinction. Most US casino welcome bonuses use the "sticky bonus" model: bonus credits remain locked in your account until rollover is cleared, then convert to withdrawable cash. A few operators (FanDuel, BetRivers) use cashable models where the bonus releases incrementally as you play through. The cashable model is mathematically friendlier — you can withdraw earlier in your play through.

Third, the game-contribution table matters more than the headline bonus size. A $1,000 bonus where blackjack contributes 10% is functionally a much smaller bonus for blackjack players than a $500 bonus where blackjack contributes 50%. Read the contribution table for your preferred games before evaluating the offer headline.

Rollover math, explained with worked examples

Let's work through the realized expected value of a typical $1,000 casino welcome bonus at 15x rollover for a slot-focused player.

Setup: $500 deposit, $500 bonus credited, total bankroll $1,000. Required playthrough: $15,000 on bonus funds (some operators apply rollover to bonus alone; others to deposit-plus-bonus, doubling the requirement). Slot RTP: 96% (a typical slot's expected return per spin).

Expected loss to RTP house edge during clearance: $15,000 × (100% − 96%) = $600 in expected loss to slot RTP during the playthrough.

Net expected position after clearing rollover: $1,000 starting bankroll − $600 expected RTP loss = $400 expected balance. Realized bonus value: $400 minus the $500 deposit = -$100 expected loss on the deposit, but you've cleared $1,000 in playable funds for $500 deposited — a 100% gross subsidy on play.

This is the practical reality of casino welcome bonuses. They subsidize your play but rarely produce withdrawable profit. The user who wins is the user who would have played that volume anyway — for them, the bonus essentially refunds half their expected RTP loss. The user who loses is the one who deposited specifically to chase the bonus and never plays after rollover clears.

The honest framing: if you're a regular casino player who plays 5-10 hours a week of slots, claiming the largest match bonus you can find produces meaningful value. If you're not — if the bonus is the entire reason you're depositing — the rollover requirement turns the bonus into a forced playthrough that will cost you more in expected loss than the bonus is worth.

Game contribution tables — why they matter

Every casino bonus has a game-contribution table that specifies what percentage of play on each game type counts toward rollover. The standard contribution structure across major US operators:

  • Slots: 100% contribution. Every $1 wagered on slots counts $1 toward rollover. Slots are the default game type for clearing casino bonuses.
  • Video poker: 20-50% contribution depending on operator and variant. Jacks-or-Better video poker typically contributes 20%; some operators exclude it entirely.
  • Blackjack: 10-25% contribution. The math is unfriendly because blackjack has the lowest house edge of any casino game; operators offset by reducing rollover contribution.
  • Roulette: 25-50% contribution. American roulette typically contributes 50%; European roulette 25-50% depending on operator.
  • Baccarat: 10-25% contribution. Similar logic to blackjack.
  • Craps: 25% contribution at most operators. Some operators explicitly exclude craps from bonus play.
  • Live dealer games: typically 10-25% of the corresponding RNG table game's contribution. Some operators count live dealer at 0% — read the T&C.
  • Progressive jackpot slots: often excluded from bonus play entirely. Operator policy varies; check the specific bonus terms.

The practical implication: if you're a blackjack player, casino welcome bonuses are dramatically less efficient than they appear. A $1,000 bonus at 15x rollover with 10% blackjack contribution requires $150,000 of blackjack play to clear — a multi-month commitment for most players. Slot-focused players clear the same bonus in $15,000 of play.

The casino bonus mistakes that cost real money

Five mistakes account for most of the value gap between nominal and realized bonus value:

  1. Claiming a bonus without intent to play through. If you deposit $500, get a $500 match, but only intend to play $200, you've stuck $300 of bonus credit in a non-cashable state and capped your withdrawal at the deposit you already had. Either claim the bonus and commit to clearing it, or skip it.
  2. Playing low-contribution games during rollover. Blackjack and baccarat at 10% contribution multiply your required playthrough by 10. If your normal game is blackjack, find an operator with high-contribution blackjack rollover or skip the bonus entirely.
  3. Going for the largest headline match. A $2,500 bonus at 15x rollover requires $37,500 of qualifying play. Most recreational players will not produce that volume. A $500 bonus at 5x rollover ($2,500 of play) is realistic and clearable for the same player.
  4. Letting the expiry window run out. Most casino bonuses expire 30 days after issuance. A bonus that's 60% cleared at day 30 expires uncleared and the bonus credits and any winnings vanish. Plan your play schedule around the expiry.
  5. Not reading the maximum-bet rule. Most casino bonuses cap maximum single-bet size during rollover clearance — typically $5-10 per spin or hand. Larger bets are flagged as bonus abuse and can void all bonus winnings retroactively. Always cap your bet sizes during clearance.

Bonus types — deep dive on each format

The five major US casino bonus formats deserve closer analysis. Each has different mathematics, different optimal usage patterns, and different ways the small print can compress real value.

The combined balance ($2X) is then subject to rollover — typically 15x for slots-eligible bonuses, 30x for "all games" bonuses with broader contribution. A $500 deposit yielding $500 bonus produces $1,000 of action. The math: you wager $15,000-$30,000 in qualifying play to clear. For a slots-only player at 96% RTP, the expected loss during clearance is $600-$1,200. Net realized bonus value: $500 deposited becomes $400-$800 expected balance after clearance — a meaningful subsidy on play but rarely a withdrawable profit.

200%+ "supersize" matches

Some operators run periodic supersize match offers — 200% or 300% of deposit. The math superficially looks better but the rollover scales proportionally. A 200% match up to $2,000 with 30x rollover produces $2,500 of action requiring $90,000 of qualifying play. The headline number is impressive; the realized expected value is smaller than the headline suggests because the playthrough requirement absorbs most of the additional bonus.

Game-contribution math — when blackjack is worth more than slots

Most casino welcome bonuses contribute slots at 100% and table games at 10-25% toward rollover. This standard structure makes slots the default clearance game. But for bettors who actually prefer table games, the math has a counterintuitive twist worth understanding.

Slots have RTP typically 92-97%, averaging 95%. House edge: 5%. Blackjack played with basic strategy has house edge approximately 0.5%, RTP 99.5%. If you're clearing a $1,000 bonus at 15x rollover ($15,000 in qualifying play):

  • On slots: $15,000 × 5% = $750 expected loss to RTP. Net bonus value: $250.
  • On blackjack at 10% contribution: $150,000 of blackjack play required to clear $15,000 of qualifying contribution. Expected loss: $150,000 × 0.5% = $750. Net bonus value: $250.

The math comes out approximately the same for both — the lower house edge of blackjack offsets the lower contribution rate. The practical difference: clearing on blackjack requires 10x the volume, which most users won't actually achieve in the 30-day window. So slots is the practical answer despite the math being equivalent in expected value.

Some operators offer table-game-friendly bonus structures with 50-100% blackjack contribution. These are rare in US legal markets but worth seeking out if you specifically prefer blackjack.

What is rollover on a casino bonus?

Rollover (also called playthrough or wagering requirement) is the number of times you must wager the bonus amount before any portion becomes withdrawable. A 15x rollover on a $1,000 bonus requires $15,000 in qualifying wagers. Different game types contribute different percentages.

Can I withdraw my casino bonus immediately?

No. Casino bonus credits cannot be withdrawn until you clear the rollover requirement. Only after the rollover is fully met can the bonus and any winnings convert to withdrawable cash.

Which casino games clear the rollover fastest?

Slots typically count 100% toward rollover and have the fastest clearance. Blackjack and baccarat usually count just 10-25%, requiring 4-10x more play to clear the same bonus.

Can I claim multiple casino bonuses?
