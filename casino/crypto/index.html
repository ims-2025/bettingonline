Tax implications of crypto-funded casino play

The IRS treats crypto-funded gambling identically to fiat-funded gambling for income reporting purposes — winnings are ordinary income, losses are itemized deductions up to the amount of winnings. But the conversion from crypto to fiat creates an additional tax event:

The crypto-to-USD conversion event. When you deposit USDC at $1.00 and the casino credits your account at $1.00, there's no taxable event. When you deposit BTC at $30,000 (cost basis) and the casino converts at $32,000 (current market), the $2,000 difference is a taxable capital gain on the BTC sale, separate from any gambling-related income on subsequent play.

For most users, this means USDC stablecoin deposits are operationally simpler than BTC/ETH deposits — no conversion gain to track. If you're depositing volatile crypto (BTC, ETH), keep records of your cost basis at deposit time; the IRS treats the deposit as a sale of the crypto.

Tax record-keeping for crypto-funded gambling

Crypto-funded gambling creates additional tax-record-keeping requirements beyond fiat-funded gambling:

The crypto cost-basis tracking

When you deposit crypto at a casino, the IRS treats this as a sale of the crypto at fair-market value at deposit time. If the crypto's value has appreciated since you acquired it, the gain is capital gains income (separate from gambling income).

Example: You bought 1 BTC at $30,000 in 2024. You deposit it at a casino in 2026 when BTC is $40,000. You realize $10,000 of capital gains income on the crypto sale, separate from any subsequent gambling activity. The capital gains tax is owed in the year of the deposit.

USDC stablecoin avoids this issue because USDC is designed to maintain a 1:1 peg with USD. No capital gain or loss on USDC deposits.

Withdrawal-side reporting

When you withdraw crypto from a casino, the operator's tax reporting (W-2G if applicable) treats the withdrawal as a USD equivalent. If you then sell the crypto for a different USD amount, you have a separate capital gain or loss event.

Record-keeping requirements

Maintain records of:

  • Crypto cost basis at deposit time (purchase price of the crypto used to fund deposits)
  • Casino deposit transactions with USD-equivalent values
  • Casino winnings (subject to W-2G if over thresholds)
  • Casino losses (deductible as itemized deductions up to amount of winnings)
  • Crypto withdrawal transactions with USD-equivalent values
  • Final crypto-to-fiat conversions and any associated capital gains
