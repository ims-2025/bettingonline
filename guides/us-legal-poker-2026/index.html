US-Legal Poker Room Directory 2026

The complete state-by-state directory of US-legal online poker rooms — which rooms are licensed where, how the multi-state shared-liquidity compact works, per-room tournament and cash-game context, and how to choose the right room for your state and format.

The state of US-legal online poker in 2026

Regulated real-money online poker is legal in six US states as of August 2026: New Jersey, Nevada, Delaware, Michigan, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia. Every other state either has no legal framework for online poker, or has legalized casino gaming but not online poker specifically. The regulated market is meaningfully smaller than sports betting (which now covers 30+ states), but the six-state footprint is enough to support a healthy competitive ecosystem — provided you're in one of those states.

The most important structural feature of US-legal poker in 2026 is the Multi-State Internet Gaming Association (MSIGA) shared-liquidity compact. Instead of each state operating in isolation (which would produce fields too thin to sustain meaningful cash-game and tournament traffic), participating states pool their player bases into shared tables and tournaments. That's what makes the difference between "poker is technically legal" and "poker is actually playable" in these states.

MSIGA — how the shared-liquidity compact works

The Multi-State Internet Gaming Association is a compact that lets state-licensed operators share players across state lines. As of August 2026, MSIGA participation:

  • Nevada — original signatory (2015)
  • Delaware — original signatory (2015)
  • New Jersey — joined 2018
  • Michigan — joined 2023
  • West Virginia — joined 2024
  • Pennsylvania — pending as of publication; expected to join in late 2026 or 2027

When two rooms operate on the same underlying platform and both hold MSIGA-participating licenses, they can pool players. The practical effect: a New Jersey resident and a Michigan resident can sit at the same cash table or enter the same tournament, playing against each other in real time. This is what makes the WSOP.com/DraftKings Poker platform's US player pool the largest in regulated US poker.

Licensed rooms by state — the 2026 directory

New Jersey (deepest market)

New Jersey is the largest and most-competitive US-legal poker market. Every major regulated operator holds a license.

  • WSOP.com / DraftKings Poker — MSIGA-connected pool with MI/NV/WV; typically the largest fields available in the US market. WSOP-branded tournament series featured prominently.
  • PokerStars NJ — participates in a separate PokerStars-brand MSIGA pool with PokerStars MI/PA. Second-largest US regulated pool.
  • BetMGM Poker — smaller pool but strong MGM Rewards integration; the choice for existing MGM property visitors.
  • Borgata Poker — runs on the BetMGM/party platform; distinct branding, same underlying platform.

Nevada (original market)

  • WSOP.com Nevada — MSIGA-connected, home of the World Series of Poker branded tournaments. The historical anchor of US-legal online poker.
  • PokerStars Nevada — active but smaller than WSOP.com in the state.

Michigan

  • WSOP / DraftKings Poker MI — MSIGA pool with NJ/NV/WV.
  • PokerStars MI — MSIGA pool with NJ/PA.
  • BetMGM Poker MI.

Pennsylvania

  • WSOP / DraftKings Poker PA — currently a state-isolated pool; MSIGA-inclusion pending.
  • PokerStars PA — MSIGA pool with NJ/MI.
  • BetMGM Poker PA.

West Virginia

  • WSOP / DraftKings Poker WV — MSIGA-connected.
  • BetMGM Poker WV.

Delaware (smallest regulated market)

Delaware operates a single state-branded skin on the WSOP.com platform (DelawarePoker.com), MSIGA-connected via the WSOP pool. The Delaware market is tiny in absolute terms but participates fully in the shared pool, so players see the same tables and tournaments as NJ/MI/NV/WV WSOP.com players.

Cash-game and tournament traffic — what the numbers actually look like

The MSIGA compact matters most for tournament traffic. A three-state combined pool (WSOP.com's NJ + MI + WV) supports Sunday major tournaments in the $50,000-$150,000 GTD range — small compared to offshore ACR's $10M-guaranteed Venom, but the largest guarantees available in the US regulated market.

Cash-game traffic on WSOP.com's shared pool typically peaks at 300-500 concurrent players across all stakes during US primetime hours (7pm-1am ET). PokerStars US pools show similar numbers. That's an order of magnitude smaller than offshore WPN (~9,000 concurrent peak) but enough to support consistent action at the $0.10/$0.25 through $2/$5 NL cash stakes that most US regulated players are focused on.

How to choose a room in your state

The room-choice decision breaks down into three questions:

  1. Which pool does the room participate in? WSOP.com pool vs PokerStars pool vs BetMGM/party pool. The pool determines the field sizes you'll see. Bigger pool = bigger fields = deeper MTT guarantees and better cash-game liquidity.
  2. What's your primary format? Tournament-focused players benefit most from the biggest-pool operator (WSOP.com in most states). Cash-game players benefit from whichever pool has the strongest traffic at your specific stake level.
  3. Is there a loyalty/rewards program you value? BetMGM Poker's MGM Rewards integration matters if you visit Vegas or MGM properties. WSOP.com's WSOP branding matters if you value bracelet-qualifier events. PokerStars' Stars Rewards is the most-refined loyalty program among US regulated rooms.

Offshore alternatives for players outside legal states

The 44 states without regulated online poker leave a large addressable market unserved by the US legal system. Some residents of those states use US-facing offshore operators (ACR, Black Chip, Ya Poker, True Poker on the Winning Poker Network; BetOnline Poker, TigerGaming, Sportsbetting.ag Poker on the Chico Poker Network). Offshore rooms are not state-licensed in the US and operate under foreign gaming licenses; players should understand the trade-offs before depositing.

What's likely to change through 2027

None of the currently-illegal-online-poker states are highly likely to legalize before 2027. Bettors in non-legal states should not plan around imminent regulated access.
